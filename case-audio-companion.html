The problem

Students of a printed Italian course book needed the audio for every unit on their phones. Apps, accounts and large downloads all lose people before they press play.

Constraints

  • Must play on cheap Android phones and on iPhones.
  • QR codes are printed, so a link can never change once a book is sold.
  • Hosting cost close to zero.

How it works

  1. QR code in the book
  2. Cloudflare Worker counts the scan (D1)
  3. Static page on GitHub Pages
  4. Unit link (#unit-07)
  5. Audio streamed from Cloudflare R2
  6. Student's phone

Decisions and why

Static pages, plus one tiny Worker

The pages have no backend to maintain, and GitHub Pages hosts them for free. The only server code is a Cloudflare Worker that counts a scan and redirects, on the free plan.

Audio on Cloudflare R2, not in the repository

Keeps large files out of git and streams them to the browser.

MP3 at 64 kbps mono

Plays on both iPhone and Android and loads quickly on mobile data. Speech doesn't need more.

Never break a printed link

When I added the new A1 and A2 pages, the original A1 page stayed at the same address, so QR codes already printed in books still work.

First-party counts, no third-party trackers

The Worker and the pages record scans, visits and which units are played in my own D1 database: time, QR code, city and device type. No IP address, name or phone number is stored, no analytics company sees the data, and the privacy page says so.

Results

  • Live and used by people who own the printed book.
  • A1 (10 units) and A2 (12 units) audio pages, each unit reachable from its own QR code.
  • Narration produced with my voice-clone kit from the teacher's own recording, with his permission.
  • A scan funnel per QR code: scanned, page opened, unit played, Messenger tapped.

Limits

  • QR codes printed before 24 October 2026 open the page directly, so they count as visits without scan details.
